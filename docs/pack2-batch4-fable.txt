Accept it strategically: use it to fund the move into certified, specialist and creative work that pays properly.
Q: Why do courts still require human translators? A: Because a translation used in legal proceedings needs someone accountable for it. Courts and government departments across the Gulf require certified translations from accredited translators, with stamps and liability attached. An engine cannot be licensed, questioned or held responsible. That accountability requirement, not sentiment, keeps certified translation human.
Q: Which specialisms pay best for Arabic-English translators? A: Legal translation leads, driven by courts, contracts and government workflows that run in Arabic. Medical and financial translation follow, where errors carry real consequences. Transcreation for brands pays well for genuinely creative translators. Conference and legal interpreting command premium daily rates. All four reward subject knowledge as much as language skill.
