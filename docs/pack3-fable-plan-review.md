# Pack 3 Consultant Report

Consultant: Fable 5, independent product and editorial consultant
Prepared for: Kim, InspireAmbitions
Date: 14 July 2026
Scope: Review of the proposed 20-role Pack 3 list for the AI Job Risk Calculator (40 to 60 occupations, 120 to 180 pages). No page drafting in this report.

## 1. Verdict: approve with edits

The proposed set is 85 per cent right. Seventeen roles are approved. Three roles should be replaced (Restaurant Manager, Barista, Fleet Coordinator) because they either overlap live pages or cannot carry 900 distinct, useful words for this audience. Three approved roles should be renamed to match how Gulf job seekers actually search. The final list of 20 is in section 8.

A repo-level observation that shaped my picks: `data/occupations.json` already links out to several slugs that have no live page, including `project-manager`, `business-analyst`, `electrician`, `driver-delivery`, `lab-technician` and `executive-chef`. Choosing Pack 3 roles that close these gaps repairs existing internal links on live pages at zero extra cost. The proposed list already closes two (Project Manager, Business Analyst); my replacements close three more.

## 2. Weak roles in the proposed list

**Restaurant Manager (reject, replace).** High overlap with the live Food and Beverage Manager page. The task lists would converge on rosters, service standards, stock and guest complaints. This is exactly the thin-variant trap the rules prohibit. The live F&B page even links to a dangling `restaurant-supervisor` slug, confirming the cluster is already crowded.

**Barista (reject, replace).** Gulf coffee culture is real, but the AI-risk question for a barista does not sustain 900 useful words, eight distinct tasks and four unique FAQs. Task-level AI exposure is a short story (ordering kiosks, one paragraph on automated brewing), and search intent for career-risk content on this title is weak. It would also be the third frontline retail-and-hospitality service role in one pack alongside Retail Sales Associate and Chef.

**Fleet Coordinator (reject, replace).** Too close to the live Logistics Coordinator page; route planning, dispatch and tracking tasks would be near-duplicates. Search volume for the exact title in the Gulf is thin, and job ads more often say Fleet Supervisor or fold the work into logistics roles.

**Watch-list (approved, but with conditions in section 7):**
- Retail Sales Associate and Store Manager together are fine only if the pages are strictly separated (frontline selling versus rostering, inventory and P&L).
- Business Analyst and Supply Chain Analyst must be firmly separated from the live Data Analyst and Financial Analyst pages; no generic "analyses data" tasks.
- Project Manager must not drift into the live Operations Manager territory.
- Dental Assistant is the weakest approved role; it survives because the Gulf angle (chairside work plus insurance pre-authorisation and sterilisation logs) is genuinely distinct, but it should be drafted late in the pack so it can be swapped if it reads thin.

## 3. Recommended replacements

1. **Electrician** (replaces Restaurant Manager). Referenced twice as a dead related-role slug on live pages. Large Gulf workforce, clear licensing and inspection workflow, and a strong low-automation story that balances a pack heavy with high-exposure desk roles. "Safe trade" content is highly shareable.
2. **Delivery Driver** (replaces Fleet Coordinator). Repairs the dangling `driver-delivery` slug. One of the largest job categories in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, with an honest and interesting AI angle: app dispatch and route algorithms already run the job, while the driving itself resists automation in Gulf conditions. High search and share potential.
3. **Security Guard** (replaces Barista). Very large Gulf workforce with a mandatory licensing pathway (SIRA in Dubai, PSBD in Abu Dhabi, and Saudi private security regulation), which gives the UAE and Saudi sections real substance. AI CCTV analytics versus physical presence is a genuinely balanced risk story.

These three also diversify the pack. As proposed, Pack 3 skews white-collar and mid-skill office roles; the replacements add trades and frontline roles that match how Gulf job seekers actually distribute across the labour market.

Mid-tier: Architect, Mechanical Engineer, HSE Officer, Physiotherapist, Store Manager, UI/UX Designer, Supply Chain Analyst. Niche but defensible: BIM Modeller, Medical Laboratory Technician, Dental Assistant, Electrician.

## 6. Renames to match Gulf search behaviour

- **Health and Safety Officer → HSE Officer.** "HSE Officer" is the dominant Gulf job-ad and search term. Keep "Health and Safety Officer" in the intro line for clarity.
- **Medical Laboratory Technologist → Medical Laboratory Technician.** Gulf ads and licensing categories use Technician far more; this also aligns with the dangling `lab-technician` related link, which should be repointed to the new slug.
- **UX/UI Designer → UI/UX Designer.** The regional search convention puts UI first; job boards in the UAE and Saudi Arabia overwhelmingly use "UI/UX Designer".
- **Chef** stays as "Chef" (the search term), but repoint the live F&B Manager page's dangling `executive-chef` link to the new Chef page.
- No other renames needed. "BIM Modeller" is the correct British spelling and matches regional usage; "Cybersecurity Analyst" is fine, though drafts should mention "cyber security" as a spelling variant once for search coverage.

## 7. Anti-thin-content rules for Pack 3

In addition to the standing rules (900 useful words, eight occupation-specific tasks, four unique FAQs, citations, three live related links):

1. **Pair separation, retail.** Retail Sales Associate and Store Manager may not share a single task or FAQ. Associate tasks are customer-facing; Store Manager tasks are rostering, inventory, compliance and P&L. If a task fits both pages, it belongs to neither.
2. **Analyst separation.** Business Analyst and Supply Chain Analyst task lists must be domain-anchored (process and requirements work versus inventory and logistics planning). The word "analyse" may not appear in a task without a named artefact or system. No task may be reusable on the live Data Analyst or Financial Analyst pages.
3. **Management separation.** Project Manager tasks must sit inside a project lifecycle (initiation to handover). Anything describing steady-state daily operations belongs to the live Operations Manager page and is banned here.
4. **Kitchen versus floor.** The Chef page covers food production only. Service, bookings, front-of-house and supplier negotiation belong to the live Food and Beverage Manager page.
5. **Diagnostics separation.** Medical Laboratory Technician covers the specimen pipeline only; no imaging content, which belongs to the live Radiographer page.
6. **Tech-stack separation.** Cloud Engineer tasks are infrastructure and platform work; nothing that fits the live Software Developer or IT Support Specialist pages. Cybersecurity Analyst may not include helpdesk or general IT support tasks.
7. **Tool-or-artefact test.** Every one of the eight tasks on every page must name a role-specific tool, document or system (for example Revit, a SIEM, a permit-to-work form, a POS terminal). A task that survives with the job title swapped out fails and must be rewritten.
8. **FAQ uniqueness across the pack.** No FAQ may appear on two Pack 3 pages with only the title changed. Every role with a Gulf licence or permit (Pharmacist, Physiotherapist, Medical Laboratory Technician, Dental Assistant, HSE Officer, Security Guard, Electrician, Architect) must carry at least one UAE- or Saudi-specific licensing FAQ.
9. **Related links go live-only.** Every related-role link on a Pack 3 page must resolve to a live page at publish time. Use this pack to repair the existing dangling references (`project-manager`, `business-analyst`, `electrician`, `driver-delivery`, `lab-technician`, `executive-chef`); do not create new dangling slugs.
10. **UAE and Saudi sections must differ.** The two country sections on a page may not be paraphrases of each other; each must contain at least one regulation, licence, workflow or market fact that is untrue of the other country.
